H₂/air 0-D ignition · strategy 5 (UR10 physics-in-loop + physics) · five-seed committee

Committee Disagreement

At every saved state of each seed's 20-ms rollout, all five best-validation models predict the next step from that same state. Their relative disagreement is compared with the actual error of that rollout, across the 18 test and 18 validation cases.

36cases (18 test + 18 val)
180rollouts (5 seeds each)
1 400query states per rollout
5members per query
best-valweights for every member

Findings

Does disagreement predict the error?

Each dot is one rollout. The x-axis is the largest relative disagreement seen before that rollout's own temperature has risen 50 K, so a running simulation can compute it without knowing τ. The y-axis is that rollout's full 20-ms temperature RMSE.

Why the slow cases fail

Both quantities are medians over the induction period (t < τ_ref), measured along the reference trajectory and expressed in units of the training increment's standard deviation. The noise is the spread of the five members' predicted step. The signal is the length of their mean step.

The noise stays near 0.005 whatever the case, but the true per-step change shrinks by almost three orders of magnitude as τ grows. Above τ ≈ 0.3 ms the signal falls to the size of the noise, which is exactly where the errors appear.

Ideas this suggests

These are ranked by how directly the evidence above supports them. None of them has been tested yet.

  1. Disagreement-gated hybrid solver

    While the rollout is still before ignition, hand each step to the stiff ODE solver whenever R exceeds about 0.3, and return to the network once the temperature has risen or R drops. On these 36 cases the gate fires only in the five slow cases, and it fires within the first 12–64 µs, well before any error appears. Accuracy there would match the reference, and the extra cost is limited to those induction periods.

  2. A coarser time step for slow induction

    The failure is a signal-to-noise problem. The true change per 2 µs step is about the size of the network's noise floor. A second model trained at a larger Δt (for example 20–50 µs), used only while R is high, takes steps that are ten or more times larger against a noise floor that should not grow as fast. This is a hypothesis: whether the noise floor really stays put at a larger Δt has to be measured.

  3. Fix the training objective, then add data

    An MAE loss in standardized increment space gives slow-induction states almost no weight, because their increments are about 1000× smaller than typical. Two options are a loss weighted by relative increment, or log-scaled radical increments. The states with high R are also where new training pairs would help most (query-by-committee active learning): the T₀ = 1000–1060 K induction region.

  4. Committee mean in the flagged regime (weakest)

    Averaging five members reduces the noise by at most about √5 ≈ 2.2×. At a signal-to-noise ratio near 1 that is probably not enough. Cases 7 and 9 also show that all members can freeze in the same way.

Definitions

Query
For a state x, each member k predicts the next state. It is then Wang-projected and its temperature recovered from enthalpy, exactly as in the strategy-5 rollouts. Pressure is excluded from the comparison.
Step vector Dk
[encode(nextk) − encode(x)] / σtrain, taken over T and the 9 BCT(Y) channels. σtrain is the standard deviation of the training-set increment, so all channels share one unit.
Relative disagreement R
‖stdk Dk‖ / (‖meank Dk‖ + ε), with ε = 10⁻³. The noise is the numerator and the signal is the length of the mean step.
Max R pre-ignition
The maximum of a 10-point (20 µs) rolling median of R along a rollout, taken while that rollout's own T is below T₀ + 50 K. It is computed online and needs no reference.
Trajectory spread
The standard deviation of T(t) across the five independent seed rollouts, set against their mean |T − Tref|.
Bad rollout
Ignition misclassified (400 K-rise criterion), or refined τ₄₀₀ error above 10%.
Sampling
0–2 ms every 2 steps and 2–20 ms every 10 steps, giving 1 400 states per rollout. Charts use about 300 log-spaced points.
